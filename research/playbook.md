## Introduction

and published.

The goal: Give any B2B SaaS team a working cold outreach system they can 
implement immediately.

## Section 1: The Core Framework

### Step 1 — Build a Targeted List

Narrow your ICP aggressively before building any list.

Bad target: "Marketing Directors"  
Good target: "Marketing Directors at B2B SaaS companies, 20-100 employees, 
raised Series A in last 18 months"

Stricter filters = more replies, fewer spam complaints.

(Source: Alex Berman, YouTube — "Generate B2B Leads with Email Outreach 
2026", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=llxqzy0yhLU)

Use Apollo.io with verified email filter. Export via Trusted Leads for 
bulk scraping at $50 per 10,000 leads. Always verify with Million Verifier 
before sending.

(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025, 
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

---

### Step 2 — Set Up Email Infrastructure

Never send cold emails from your main company domain. Buy 3-5 variation 
domains. Set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC on every domain.

Warm up domains for minimum 30 days before sending. Start at 10-20 emails 
per day, increase gradually to 25-50 per mailbox per day.

(Source: Alex Berman, YouTube — "Generate B2B Leads with Email Outreach 
2026", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=llxqzy0yhLU)

Use Google Workspace mailboxes — currently highest inbox placement at 87.2%.

(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

---

### Step 3 — Write the Email

Follow the Triple Tap Framework:

**Tap 1 — Subject line + first sentence (get the OPEN)**
- 3-7 words
- Never signal the sale
- Use curiosity or a question
- Example: "Sending cold emails?" or "Question, [First Name]"

**Tap 2 — Email body (get them to READ)**
- Max 6 sentences, 6th grade reading level
- Name their problem
- One sentence on how you solve it
- One credibility line (case study, client name, result)

**Tap 3 — CTA (get a one-word REPLY)**
- "Want me to send it over?"
- "Worth a quick look?"
- They should be able to reply with just "Yes"

(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

Use the CCQ Formula for LinkedIn outreach openers:
Compliment + Commonality + Question — before any pitch.

(Source: Jack Reamer, LinkedIn post — June 25, 2026,
https://www.linkedin.com/in/jackreamer/)

---

### Step 4 — Send Sequence

Use a 3-email sequence maximum:

- Day 0: Email 1 (main pitch, plain text only, no links)
- Day 3: Email 2 (simple bump in same thread)
- Day 7: Email 3 (dump — add case studies, links, video)

Stop after 3. More emails = higher spam complaints.

(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

(Source: Alex Berman, YouTube — "Generate B2B Leads with Email Outreach 
2026", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=llxqzy0yhLU)

---

### Step 5 — Follow Up on Replies Fast

Reply within 5-10 minutes of an interested reply. Speed to lead increases 
conversion by 60%.

Do not info-dump. Goal of first reply is to book a call — not to explain 
everything. Leave a curiosity gap.

Always include a clear next step: "Do you have 15 minutes Thursday? 
Here's the link."

(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

## Section 2: Where Experts Disagree

### Disagreement 1 — How Many Follow-Up Emails?

**Alex Berman recommends:**
4 emails over 2 weeks — Day 0, Day 3, Day 7, Day 14. Each follow-up 
needs a different angle, not just "bumping this up."
(Source: Alex Berman, YouTube — "Generate B2B Leads with Email Outreach 
2026", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=llxqzy0yhLU)

**Jay Feldman recommends:**
Maximum 3 emails. Stop after 3 because more emails increase spam 
complaints faster than they increase replies.
(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

**My take: I side with Jay Feldman.**
In 2026, spam filters are smarter and buyers are more defensive. A 4th 
email rarely converts someone who ignored 3 — it mostly damages your 
sender reputation. 3 emails with strong angles is better than 4 emails 
with diminishing returns.

---

### Disagreement 2 — Personalization: How Much Is Too Much?

**Jack Reamer recommends:**
Ultra-personalized first lines on every email. Reference something 
specific — a blog post, a product launch, a recent interview. Real 
1-to-1 personalization is his entire brand.
(Source: Jack Reamer, LinkedIn post — June 25, 2026,
https://www.linkedin.com/in/jackreamer/)

**Jay Feldman recommends:**
Personalization is often overrated and can hurt campaigns. Most people 
do AI personalization wrong — if it reads as AI-generated, it kills 
reply rates. Fundamentals (targeting + offer) matter more than fancy 
personalization.
(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

**My take: Partial agreement with both.**
For small, high-value lists (under 500 leads) — Jack Reamer's approach 
wins. For large-scale campaigns (10,000+ leads) — Jay Feldman is right. 
Use personalization only when you can do it accurately. Bad 
personalization is worse than no personalization.

---

### Disagreement 3 — Lead Magnet vs. Direct Pitch

**Jack Reamer recommends:**
The Workshop Funnel — invite prospects to a free live training instead 
of asking for a 15-minute call. This gets 3-4% reply rates vs 1-3% 
traditional, and lets you sell one-to-many.
(Source: Jack Reamer, YouTube — "The Best Cold Email Strategy in 2025",
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nJIKepv7z74)

**Jay Feldman recommends:**
Reverse Lead Magnets — make the prospect think you spent personal time 
preparing something specifically for them. AI generates it at scale but 
it feels custom. Higher perceived value than a generic workshop invite.
(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

**My take: Depends on your stage.**
Workshop Funnel works better for established experts who already have 
credibility and an audience. Reverse Lead Magnets work better for 
beginners who need to stand out without social proof. If you are just 
starting, go with Reverse Lead Magnets first.

## Section 3: What I Rejected and Why

### Rejected Idea 1 — AI-Written Full Emails

Jay Feldman, Alex Berman, and Michel Lieben all mention using AI to 
write entire cold email sequences.

**Why I rejected it:**
Every expert who mentions this also warns against it. Jay Feldman 
specifically says: "Do not trust any AI to write your emails." 
Michel Lieben built a $4M agency and still emphasizes human editing 
of AI output. If the top practitioners are warning against fully 
AI-written emails, it is a shortcut that destroys results. AI can help 
with spin tax, spam word checking, and subject line variations — but 
the core copy needs a human voice.

(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)
(Source: Michel Lieben, YouTube — "How I built a $4M/year cold email 
agency", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RSeT3pSjZoQ)

---

### Rejected Idea 2 — Extended Sequences (5-7 Emails)

Some frameworks suggest sending 5 to 7 follow-up emails to maximize 
chances of getting a reply.

**Why I rejected it:**
Jay Feldman only recommends extended sequences for very small total 
addressable markets where you have run out of new leads. For standard 
B2B SaaS outreach with a large market, sending more than 3 emails 
increases spam complaints faster than it increases replies. The math 
does not work in favor of volume over quality at this stage of the 
email landscape in 2026.

(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

## Section 4: My Original Ideas

### Original Idea — The "Soft No Follow-Up" System

None of the experts I researched had a structured system for handling 
soft no replies at scale.

A soft no is when someone replies with something like:
"Not interested right now, thanks."
"We already have a solution."
"Not the right time."

Most SDRs archive these and move on. My idea: treat every soft no as 
a warm lead in a separate nurture sequence.

**How it would work:**
1. Tag all soft no replies in your CRM separately
2. Wait 60-90 days
3. Send a single follow-up with a new angle — different pain point, 
   new case study, or relevant industry news
4. No pitch. Just: "Saw this and thought of our conversation. 
   Relevant to what you mentioned?"

**Why it could work:**
Soft no replies already engaged with you — they replied. That puts 
them ahead of 98% of your list who never responded. Their situation 
changes. Budgets open up. Vendors disappoint them. The person who 
said "not now" in January is a completely different buyer in Q3. 
Most competitors abandon these leads entirely, which means zero 
competition in this follow-up window.

Jay Feldman mentions replying to soft nos in his masterclass but does 
not build it into a structured system. This is the gap I am filling.

(Reference: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)

## Section 5: Weaknesses of This Playbook

**1. Built on content, not personal campaign data**
Every recommendation here comes from expert content — YouTube videos 
and LinkedIn posts. None of it has been tested by me personally in a 
live campaign. What works for an agency with 890 mailboxes may not 
work the same way for a solo founder with 10.

**2. The market changes fast**
Jay Feldman himself says: "By the time you watch this, some of what 
I'm saying may be irrelevant." Cold email best practices shift 
quarterly. This playbook reflects the state of the field in mid-2026 
and may need significant updates by early 2027.

**3. Assumes B2B SaaS with a clear ICP**
This playbook works best when you know exactly who you are targeting. 
If your ICP is unclear, or your offer is too broad, none of these 
tactics will produce consistent results. The list-building section 
especially depends on having precise filters.

**4. The "Soft No Follow-Up" idea is untested**
My original idea in Section 4 is logical but unproven. I have not 
run this system. It is based on reasoning from expert frameworks, not 
real data. It could fail if the timing or messaging is off.

**5. No coverage of cold calling or SMS**
This playbook focuses entirely on email and LinkedIn. Multi-channel 
outreach including phone and SMS is mentioned by experts but not 
covered here. A complete cold outreach system would need those 
channels too.

## Section 6: Who I Would NOT Recommend Following and Why

### Not Recommended: Jay Feldman (for beginners)

Let me be clear — Jay Feldman is one of the most knowledgeable people 
in cold email. His masterclass is genuinely excellent and contains 
more practical detail than most paid courses.

However, I would not recommend him as a starting point for beginners 
for the following reasons:

**1. Heavy promotion of his own services**
Throughout his content, Jay consistently promotes his own setup 
service ($3/mailbox), his coaching program (Galadon Gold), his school 
community, and his affiliate links. The advice is real, but it is 
difficult for a beginner to separate "what is genuinely best practice" 
from "what benefits Jay's business." For example, he recommends his 
own mailbox setup service as the best option — which may be true, but 
he profits from that recommendation.

**2. Complexity too early**
Jay's content moves from beginner to advanced very quickly. His 
masterclass covers Clay AI, N8N automations, Apify scrapers, 
Supabase knowledge bases, and autonomous BDR systems. For someone 
just starting with cold email, this level of complexity creates 
paralysis rather than action. You end up learning tools instead of 
sending emails.

**My recommendation:**
Start with Michel Lieben (ColdIQ) or Alex Berman to understand the 
fundamentals clearly. Return to Jay Feldman once you have run at 
least one real campaign and understand the basics from personal 
experience.

(Source: Jay Feldman, YouTube — Cold Email Masterclass 2025,
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDGcd1JoJV0)
